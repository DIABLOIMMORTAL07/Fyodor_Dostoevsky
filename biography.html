From Moscow to Siberia, a life shaped by hardship, observation and literature.

1821 — 1881

The experiences that shaped a literary conscience.

Fyodor Mikhailovich Dostoevsky was born in Moscow in 1821. His life brought him into contact with poverty, political repression, imprisonment, illness and financial difficulty—experiences that profoundly informed his fiction.

He became one of the central figures of Russian literature, known for psychologically intense novels and characters wrestling with moral and spiritual questions.

A CHRONOLOGY

Moments in a life

1821 — 1881
1821

Born in Moscow

Born on 11 November (30 October Old Style), into a family whose father was a physician.

1838

Studies in St. Petersburg

Entered the Nikolayev Military Engineering Institute while his interest in literature continued to grow.

1846

A literary debut

His first novel, Poor Folk, appeared and brought him early attention.

1849

Arrest and sentence

After involvement with the Petrashevsky Circle, he was arrested. A death sentence was commuted to penal servitude.

1850–54

Years in Siberia

He endured imprisonment in Omsk, followed by compulsory military service.

1866

Two major novels

Crime and Punishment and The Gambler were published.

1868–69

The Idiot

Written largely during his years abroad, the novel explores goodness and innocence in society.

1880–81

Final work and legacy

The Brothers Karamazov was completed in 1880. Dostoevsky died in St. Petersburg in 1881.

PLACE & MEMORY

St. Petersburg

The city became a recurring presence in Dostoevsky’s fiction: crowded streets, cramped rooms and social contrasts form the atmosphere of several major novels.
